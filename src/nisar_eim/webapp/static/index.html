NASA–ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar · Level-2 GUNW

Radar interferometry, made inspectable.

NISAR measures the distance between a satellite and the ground twice, eleven days apart. Where the surface stays coherent, the phase difference between those two radar echoes records how that distance changed. This application lets you explore those measurements at full precision — and see exactly where they stop being trustworthy.

What this data is — and is not

Three layers, three different kinds of statement.

Phase

Unwrapped interferometric phase

A measurement of the phase difference between two radar acquisitions, in radians. It responds to changes in range, but also to the ionosphere, the troposphere, orbit geometry and unwrapping offsets. Its absolute level is arbitrary.

Not a displacement map in metres. Not vertical uplift.

Coherence

Interferometric coherence magnitude

A dimensionless 0–1 measure of how similar the two observations are. It tells you where the phase relationship is meaningful: vegetation, water and change decorrelate.

A quality indicator, not a measurement of motion.

Mask

Validity and quality layers

The product mask encodes water, reference and secondary subswaths and RSLC anomalies; connected components record how the phase was unwrapped across regions.

“Valid” means measurable — not necessarily scientifically usable.
